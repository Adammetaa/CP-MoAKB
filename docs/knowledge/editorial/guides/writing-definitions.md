# Guide: Writing Definitions

Status: Active
Version: 1.0

## Purpose
Produce neutral, scoped, evidence-supported candidate definitions.
## Scope
Concept definitions and their distinctions.
## Out of Scope
Definitions MUST NOT diagnose, advise management, or create identity.
## Authority
Subordinate to the [handbook](../knowledge-editorial-handbook.md) and [KAS-002](../../KAS-002-knowledge-record-standard.md).
## Definitions
A definition states what a concept is; an exclusion states what it is not.
## Responsibilities
Author drafts; Scientific and Terminology Reviewers test meaning and language.
## Procedure
State the category and distinguishing characteristics; identify adjacent concepts;
add scope and evidence; remove circular wording, diagnostic claims, and advice.
## Required Inputs
Candidate identity, evidence, adjacent concepts, scope, and accepted term status.
## Required Outputs
Definition, distinctions, scope, exclusions, evidence links, and open questions.
## Review Points
Clarity, non-circularity, distinction, neutrality, support, and bounded scope.
## Failure Modes
Label-as-definition, universal claims, hidden uncertainty, diagnosis, or recommendation.
## Examples
“Fictional Concept Alpha is a synthetic review object distinguished by its declared editorial-only scope.”
## Non-examples
“Alpha is Alpha and should be treated immediately” MUST NOT pass.
## Escalation
Scientific ambiguity goes to Scientific Review; term ambiguity to Terminology Review.
## Audit Requirements
Retain drafts, evidence, comments, decisions, and accepted wording.
## Change Control
Meaning changes require re-review and versioned history.
## Future Considerations
Domain-specific style guidance MAY be proposed separately.
