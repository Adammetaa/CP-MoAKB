# Guide: Writing Scope and Exclusions

Status: Active
Version: 1.0

## Purpose
Bound what a candidate means and explicitly does not mean.
## Scope
Included and excluded meaning, context, jurisdiction, crop, organism, growth stage, and time.
## Out of Scope
Scope MUST NOT invent applicability or establish a taxonomy.
## Authority
Subordinate to the [handbook](../knowledge-editorial-handbook.md), KAS-002, and ADR-008.
## Definitions
**Included meaning** is supported applicability; **excluded meaning** prevents overreach.
## Responsibilities
Author states boundaries; reviewers test them against evidence and authority.
## Procedure
Declare context; list included meaning; list exclusions; state jurisdiction and
temporal limits; record crop, organism, or growth-stage applicability only where
evidenced; mark unknowns without filling them.
## Required Inputs
Evidence scope, authority scope, intended concept, and known adjacent meanings.
## Required Outputs
Reviewable inclusion, exclusion, context, jurisdiction, domain, and time statements.
## Review Points
Evidence fit, authority fit, clarity, omissions, and unsupported generalization.
## Failure Modes
Universal scope, absent exclusions, jurisdiction leakage, or inferred domain coverage.
## Examples
A fictional candidate MAY be limited to “editorial demonstration context only.”
## Non-examples
“Applies everywhere” without evidence MUST NOT pass.
## Escalation
Unresolved domain scope goes to a competent Domain or Scientific Reviewer.
## Audit Requirements
Retain scope changes, supporting evidence, decisions, and exclusions.
## Change Control
Scope expansion is material and requires new review.
## Future Considerations
Pilot-specific guidance requires separate authorization.
