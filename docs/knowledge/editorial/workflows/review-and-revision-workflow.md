# Review and Revision Workflow

Status: Active
Version: 1.0

## Purpose
Coordinate competent, auditable specialist reviews and controlled revisions.

## Scope
Scientific, evidence, terminology, ontology, governance, rights, and publication-readiness review.

## Out of Scope
No review MAY decide outside competence or make publication authorization automatic.

## Authority
This workflow applies the [handbook](../knowledge-editorial-handbook.md),
[KGS-002](../../governance/KGS-002-roles-and-responsibilities.md),
[KGS-003](../../governance/KGS-003-review-process.md), and KGS-004.

## Definitions
**Required revision** is a blocking change; **defer** awaits identified evidence or
authority; **recuse** removes a reviewer because competence or independence is absent.

## Responsibilities
Managing Editor verifies assignments. Each reviewer declares competence and
conflicts, asks scope-specific questions, records reasons, and MUST recuse when necessary.

## Procedure
1. Confirm complete inputs, review type, competence, independence, and authority.
2. Scientific review checks scientific meaning and neutrality; evidence review
   checks fidelity and traceability; terminology review checks term governance;
   ontology review checks layers and semantics; governance review checks authority
   and process; rights review checks permitted use; publication review checks gates.
3. Select only: approve; approve with required revision; return for revision;
   reject; defer; escalate; or recuse.
4. State review questions, findings, revision requirements, rejection conditions,
   and escalation path. The Author responds item by item.
5. Re-review every material revision; preserve prior decisions and dissent.

## Required Inputs
Candidate package, review brief, authority versions, competence declaration,
conflict-of-interest disclosure, and earlier findings.

## Required Outputs
Decision, reasons, required revisions, unresolved issues, reviewer role,
competence, recusal status, and audit record.

## Review Points
Completeness, competence, independence, evidence, terminology, semantics, rights,
authority, lifecycle, conflicts, and closure of required revisions.

## Failure Modes
Rubber-stamp approval, out-of-scope decision, undisclosed conflict, erased dissent,
untracked revision, ambiguous decision, or one specialist substituting for another.

## Examples
An Evidence Reviewer MAY approve traceability while explicitly deferring scientific meaning.

## Non-examples
A Managing Editor MUST NOT self-assign every specialist approval without qualifications.

## Escalation
Unresolved disagreement and appeals follow KGS-003 and KGS-004; rights blockers
remain blockers; authority conflicts stop the affected review.

## Audit Requirements
Retain inputs, questions, competence, disclosures, decisions, reasons, revisions,
responses, recusals, escalation, appeal, and final disposition.

## Change Control
Changes require KGS review and MUST NOT redistribute role authority.

## Future Considerations
Review aids MAY be proposed, but human accountability MUST remain explicit.
