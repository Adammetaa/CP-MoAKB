# Fictional Workspace Journeys

**Status:** Illustrative design examples
**Version:** 0.1.0

## Purpose and Scope

These journeys test whether the Knowledge Workspace Blueprint can explain governed authoring and review handoffs. Every identifier, source, claim, organization, and event is fictional and intentionally domain-neutral.

## Out of Scope

The examples contain no agricultural master data, crop, disease, insect, chemical, institution, regulation, real source, diagnosis, recommendation, scientific conclusion, schema, or working behavior.

## Authority and Audience

The examples are non-normative. The Knowledge Constitution, KAS, KGS, ADR, RAS, Design Freeze, Source Policy, and Publication Boundary remain authoritative. Readers should use these examples to evaluate interface concepts, not as knowledge records.

## Example Rules

- Fictional identifiers use the `FICTION` marker.
- Human judgment is explicit; no step is automated or inferred.
- Evidence, claims, findings, versions, roles, authority, and decisions remain distinct.
- Acceptance and publication remain separate.
- Empty or failed steps preserve uncertainty and route to governed human action.

## Accessibility and Audit Expectations

Any future rendering must provide linear, textual alternatives and make roles, status, errors, and handoffs perceivable without color. Each consequential handoff described in the journeys would require an audit event under KGS.

## Non-example

A realistic worked example naming a crop, field condition, treatment, regulator, or actual paper would be out of scope and could be mistaken for governed knowledge.

## Future Implementation Considerations

Future usability studies may create new fictional scenarios under the same safety rules. No sample should become seeded data.

## Change Control

Examples require documentation review and must remain fictional, non-normative, and free of agricultural content.
