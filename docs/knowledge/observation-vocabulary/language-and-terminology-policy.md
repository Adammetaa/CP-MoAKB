# Observation Language and Terminology Policy

Status: Active

Version: 1.0

## Purpose

This policy governs multilingual and local-language treatment for observation
terminology, with Thai-first compatibility and explicit English technical
alignment.

## Thai-first Compatibility

Thai labels and definitions MUST be authorable, reviewable, and publishable as
first-class expressions. English technical alignment SHOULD be maintained where
it assists interoperability, but English MUST NOT silently control Thai meaning.
Each language form MUST be reviewed in its own linguistic and domain context.

Thai-first compatibility does not authorize a production term set. It defines
how future candidate language MUST be governed.

## Distinct Language Relationships

Reviewers MUST distinguish:

- **translation:** a target-language rendering reviewed for meaning in context;
- **transliteration:** a script conversion that does not establish semantic
  equivalence;
- **synonymy:** shared meaning within a declared language and scope;
- **equivalence:** an explicit mapping claim with a stated strength and basis;
- **alternative label:** an accepted but non-preferred expression; and
- **local expression:** a community-, farmer-, or region-associated form whose
  usage and attribution are preserved.

None of these relationships MUST be inferred from spelling, automated
translation, co-occurrence, or apparent similarity.

## Local and Farmer Expressions

Local and farmer expressions MUST be preserved with respect, provenance, locale,
speaker context where ethically appropriate, and usage notes. They MUST NOT be
silently normalized into technical terminology. A local expression MUST NOT be
treated as a diagnosis, causal identification, or exact synonym unless the
specific relationship has passed the appropriate reviews.

Where a local expression combines observation and interpretation, reviewers
MUST preserve the reported form for traceability while separating any neutral
observation meaning from the interpretive claim.

## Label Preference

Preferred-label decisions MUST state language, locale, audience, scope, and
review authority. A preferred Thai label and a preferred English label MAY have
different grammatical structure or scope notes. Preference in one language MUST
NOT imply exact cross-language equivalence.

## Accessibility and Clarity

Definitions SHOULD use clear, direct language suitable for human review.
Specialist terms MUST be explained or linked to governed concepts. Labels MUST
avoid stigmatizing, misleading, or unnecessarily inaccessible language.

## Automated Assistance

Machine translation or other automation MAY propose candidates only under
separate authorization. It MUST NOT approve preferred labels, equivalence,
synonyms, definitions, or publication eligibility. Human reviewers remain
accountable.

All language decisions remain subject to
