# Observation Vocabulary Purpose and Boundary

Status: Active

Version: 1.0

## Purpose

This document defines what an observation vocabulary may describe and the
interpretive claims it must never encode.

## Governing Boundary

Observation terminology MUST describe morphology or perception first: what was
seen, measured, counted, heard, imaged, sampled, or otherwise recorded. It MUST
remain neutral about why the observation exists and what should happen next.

An observation vocabulary MAY govern terms for:

- observable entities;
- structures and locations;
- observable or measurable properties;
- states and presence or absence;
- counts and qualitative quantities;
- spatial distributions;
- temporal patterns;
- visible or measurable changes;
- uncertainty expressions; and
- references to observation context.

## Prohibited Semantic Payload

An observation term MUST NOT inherently assert:

- a causal agent;
- a named diagnosis;
- disease identity;
- pest identity inferred from damage;
- nutrient deficiency;
- pesticide need;
- treatment or treatment efficacy;
- resistance;
- safety or regulatory status;
- prediction; or
- recommendation.

These meanings belong to separate governed layers. A familiar domain label
MUST be rejected or qualified when its ordinary use silently embeds any such
claim.

## Observation and Evidence

An Observation is source-adjacent descriptive input. Evidence is a separately
reviewed relationship between material and a precise claim. A vocabulary term
MUST NOT assign evidence level, establish truth, prove a claim, or authorize
promotion from Observation to Evidence. See
[Evidence Levels](../../EVIDENCE_LEVELS.md) and the
[Observation Knowledge Architecture](../observation/README.md).

## Crop Independence

Core meanings MUST be valid without a crop identity. Crop, cultivar, growth
stage, organ, environment, method, time, and location MAY be supplied as
separate context when relevant. They MUST NOT be smuggled into a neutral term's
definition.

## Boundary Tests

A candidate is inside scope only when reviewers can answer yes to all of the
following:

1. Does it describe a perceivable or measurable feature rather than a cause?
2. Can its meaning be understood without selecting a diagnosis or hypothesis?
3. Does it remain valid across crops when its context is stated separately?
4. Can another reviewer reproduce the interpretation from the definition and
   scope notes?
5. Can it be used without recommending a decision or action?

Failure of any test MUST return the candidate for revision or rejection.

## Non-example

Treating a damage pattern as proof of a particular organism collapses
Observation into Hypothesis or Diagnosis and MUST NOT be accepted as neutral
observation terminology.
