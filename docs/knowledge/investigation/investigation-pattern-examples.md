# Investigation Pattern Examples

Status: Active

Version: 1.0

## Use of Examples

These fictional patterns illustrate architecture only. They are not question-bank
entries, controlled terms, agricultural records, diagnoses, recommendations,
collection protocols, or executable workflows.

## Pattern One: Missing Localization

**Observation:** linear translucent streak observed on leaf blade.

**Information State:** lesion initiation point is `not recorded` in the bounded
observation account.

**Information Gap:** lesion initiation point not recorded.

**Investigation Focus:** spatial origin of the recorded streak.

**Investigation Question:** where on the leaf did the lesion first become visible?

**Evidence Need:** close image showing lesion origin and relationship to leaf veins.

**Hypothesis Candidates:** a human author may record multiple provisional
explanations, such as an explanation associated with the observed tissue path
and a competing explanation associated with a localized external event. These
placeholders intentionally do not name a real condition, causal agent, or diagnosis.

**Differential Comparison:** reviewers may compare the candidates using the
explicit criterion “relationship of the recorded streak to visible vein
structure.” The required image may be supporting, challenging, missing, or
conflicting for each candidate after separate evidence assessment. No candidate
is ranked or selected.

**Human Review:** the reviewer checks that `not recorded` was not converted to
`not observed`, that the question is neutral, and that the candidates remain
provisional. The gap may remain open.

## Pattern Two: Conflicting Temporal Accounts

Two attributable reports describe different times of first visibility. The
Information State is `conflicting`, not an averaged time and not automatically
the newest report. The gap concerns temporal history. A neutral question asks
each reporter to clarify the reference event and time basis. An Evidence Need
describes attributable, time-bounded records. A procedural Investigation Finding
may state that the conflict remains after review; it does not establish cause or severity.

## Pattern Three: Bounded Non-detection

A reviewer examines one image angle for a particular visible feature and does
not detect it. `Not observed` is limited to that method, image, time, and feature.
It must not be generalized to absence on the specimen. A need for another view
may remain open. Lack of that view is missing evidence, not evidence that the
feature is absent.

## Pattern Four: Language and Identity

A Thai local name is reported for an observed organism. The name may be retained
as `reported` with language and provenance. A verification question may ask what
identity basis accompanied the report, and an Evidence Need may describe a
reviewable specimen image or authoritative identification record. The local name
does not become confirmed scientific identity and does not select a candidate.

## Prohibited Patterns

- diagnosing automatically from lesion shape;
- recommending pesticide or treatment from an Observation or gap;
- ranking a candidate through an unexplained score;
- converting missing information into a negative Observation;
- treating absence of evidence as evidence of absence;
- treating a local name as confirmed scientific identity;
- generating candidates from image classification or terminology matches; and
- promoting an Investigation Finding to Claim, Diagnosis, Recommendation, or Decision.

## Review Reminder

Example sequence does not imply state transition. A future interface may show
the concepts in another order, but every relationship, candidate, assessment,
and review remains an explicit human-governed act.
