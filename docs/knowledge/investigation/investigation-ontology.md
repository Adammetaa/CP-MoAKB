# Investigation Ontology

Status: Active

Version: 1.0

## Ontological Position

Investigation is a governed inquiry structure situated after recorded
Observation and before authored Diagnosis or Recommendation. It coordinates
epistemic roles without becoming an Observation, Evidence Object, Knowledge
Object, Claim, Hypothesis truth assertion, Diagnosis, or action authority.

## Core Structure

An Investigation concerns a bounded subject and may organize one or more
Investigation Cases. A case establishes an Investigation Focus. Recorded
information is described through distinct Information States; an unresolved
requirement is an Information Gap. A gap may raise neutral Investigation
Questions and require Evidence Needs. Human authors may introduce provisional
Hypothesis Candidates, group plausible competitors in a Differential Set, and
compare them using explicit Comparison Criteria. The case may record an
Investigation Finding and a Review State.

These roles are conceptual. They prescribe neither records nor cardinalities.

## Authored, Not Inferred

Every focus, state, gap, question, need, candidate, differential membership,
criterion, evidence assessment, finding, and review conclusion must be explicitly
authored or affirmed by an accountable human. Co-occurrence, graph paths,
terminology matches, interface sequence, and missing values create no semantic
relationship.

## Epistemic Distinctions

- Information State describes the status of information, not the world in
  general.
- Information Gap expresses a missing or unresolved investigation requirement,
  not a negative finding.
- Investigation Question requests information without asserting its answer.
- Evidence Need describes relevant evidence sought, not evidence already held.
- Hypothesis Candidate preserves a challengeable possibility, not a diagnosis.
- Differential Set preserves competitors; it is not a ranking.
- Investigation Finding records an investigation result in its own bounded role.
- Review State records review status, not scientific or diagnostic truth.

## Investigation Finding Categories

A finding may be descriptive, procedural, evidentiary, or comparative. For
example, it may record that two reports conflict, that a requested view remains
unavailable, that material was reviewed for a criterion, or that candidates
remain unresolved. The category and basis must be explicit. No finding is
silently promoted to Claim, Diagnosis, Recommendation, or Decision.

## Lifecycle Relevance

An Investigation may be opened, scoped, revised, reviewed, left unresolved,
reopened, or closed under future governance. These words describe conceptual
relevance only. Closure means no more work is currently authorized or required
within the case; it does not certify truth, completeness, diagnosis, or action.
Historical states, rejected interpretations, conflicts, and review rationales
remain traceable.

## Implementation Neutrality

The ontology selects no identifier, ontology language, schema, graph model,
serialization, storage, API, code class, validation mechanism, or UI. Any future
mapping must preserve explicit authorship, boundaries, conflict, history,
localization provenance, and the absence of automatic inference or promotion.
